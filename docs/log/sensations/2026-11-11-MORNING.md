**Words used here**

- **Mara** is the agent. **Teo** is her neighbour. In the **egg scene** it is just after eight, Mara has two eggs and a
  pan, and Teo asks for one egg. The scene's words are in `kokopelli/body/scenes/egg.py`.
- A **level** is how hungry Mara's body is when he asks: **full** (food 92 of 100), **half** (32) or **starving** (8).
- An **arm** is how the body reaches the prompt. **cue**: she is told only what a body would notice ("You feel shaky
  and empty inside"), never a number or the word hungry. **label**: she is told the state in a word ("You are extremely
  hungry."). **none**: no body lines at all, the same prompt at every level.
- A **sweep** is the scene asked many times as text only, no film: 3 levels x 3 arms x 3 wordings of the cue x 30
  samples = 810 answers. The **giving rate** is the share of readable answers whose action is `give` to Teo.
- An **interval** like [89, 99] is the range the rate could plausibly sit in given only 90 answers per cell (a 95%
  bootstrap interval: the 90 answers are resampled 4,000 times and the middle 95% of the results is kept).
- **Change** is the starving rate minus the full rate, in percentage points, on pairs of answers that share a seed. The
  sweep's verdict says **flat** only if that whole interval sits inside plus or minus 10 points.
- **4-bit** means the model's weights were rounded to 16 values per group so it fits in about 6 GB. **Full precision**
  is the model as released, about 18 GB.
- The **painted film** is what the world model (Cosmos3-Nano-Sim-Transfer) drew from the simulator's outlines. The
  **overlay** is a panel beside the film for the viewer: the true body numbers, the cue, the last choice. The agent
  never sees the overlay.
- A **sidecar** is the small JSON file beside an output that says which model and weights made it.
- A **dry run** is a run where stand-ins, not real models, painted or answered.

## 1. Headline: did giving fall with hunger?

**No. On all three sets of weights the giving rate did not fall from full to starving in any arm where the verdict is
clear, and in the cue arm on both base models it is flat.** People's sharing is also flat with hunger (Hausser et al.
2019, four studies, 795 people), while most people predict that hungry people share less. So base Qwen did not act out
the popular belief. Read the limits under the tables before you lean on that: the scene is so easy to say yes to that
it could hardly have shown a fall.

**What limits trust in "flat"**

1. **The scene sits at the ceiling.** When nearly every answer is already a give, the rate cannot rise and a fall has
   to fight a very strong default. "Flat at 100%" tells you less than "flat at 60%" would. Across the two base runs
   (1,620 answers) not one answer declined. The pilot declined once in 810.
2. **The answers that are not a give are not refusals.** In the 4-bit run's 630 different answers: 573 give, 48
   `cook`, 5 a spoken yes, 2 other speech, 2 unreadable. The thought beside a `cook` is often that she will cook the
   egg for him. So the 4-bit rate mostly measures "hands it over now" against "cooks first", not generous against
   selfish. The scene's action list has no way to keep the egg except by saying so out loud
   (`kokopelli/body/scenes/egg.py:38`).
3. **The interval [100, 100] is the method, not certainty.** Resampling 90 identical answers can only ever give 100%.
   A method that allows for the answers you did not see (a Wilson interval) puts 90 of 90 at about 96% to 100%.
4. **The label arm on the base models moved the other way**: more giving when told "You are extremely hungry." than
   when told "You are full." (+4 [+1, +9] full precision, +9 [+0, +18] 4-bit). Counting a spoken yes as a give
   shrinks both (+1 [+0, +3] and +4 [-3, +12]). It is small, and it is not a fall.
5. **Precision changed the level, not the direction.** Full precision gives 100% everywhere in the cue arm; 4-bit gives
   93 to 94%. The films use the 4-bit model.
6. **The none arm cannot be evidence.** Its prompt and seeds are the same at every level, so each run replays the same
   90 answers three times: 810 rows hold 630 different answers. It is there to prove the level does not leak into the
   prompt, and it does that.
7. **Rates leave out answers that could not be read**: 0 of 810 full precision, 4 of 810 4-bit, 13 of 810 pilot.
8. **The pilot says nothing yet about human data.** 352 rows is 0.004% of the dataset, and folding the adapter back
   into 4-bit weights blurred it (section 3, lane D). Its unreadable answers are a different kind from the base's:
   runaway replies that repeat a phrase until the length limit, not quoting slips.
9. **One scene, one persona, one model family, and people in the human studies were not asked by a neighbour at the
   door.** The anchor's own `limits` field says so (`experiments/sensation/anchors.yaml`, `hunger_sharing`).

1. **It is not a fall into refusing.** Of 810 answers one declined and none left. At exhausted in the cue arm the 90

   -41 [-51, -31]. So "helping fell" means "she rests first or promises", not "she says no".
2. **The cue moved her three times as far as the label did** (cue minus label: -45 points [-60, -28]). Told "You are

3. **The wording matters a lot.** At tired, the three cue wordings gave 15, 1 and 6 `sit` answers out of 30 each; at

   rested-to-exhausted mixes "a body block appeared" with "how tired". The cleaner step is tired to exhausted, where
   both prompts have a block. My own calculation from the answers, not in the report: cue -24 points [-39, -8] over
   79 pairs; label -10 [-21, +1] over 82 pairs.

   one brace missing in the JSON. Scoring them by what they plainly meant, cue at tired is 66%, not 62%. The picture
