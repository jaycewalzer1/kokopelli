## What the data turned out to be

Three things the plan assumed that the file doesn't bear out. All measured with `prepare.py survey` on every session.

1. **76 experiment ids from 46 studies, not 160.** The card says 160 experiments; the public training file has 76
   distinct `experiment` values (`badham2017deficits/exp1.csv` and so on). Its other two totals match the card exactly:
   60,092 sessions and 10,681,650 choices. So nothing is missing; the paper counts experiments more finely than the
   file names them.
2. **There are no giving games in it.** No dictator, ultimatum, public-goods or trust game, and no experiment that
   manipulates or asks about hunger. It is bandits, risky choice, category learning, memory and planning. The words
   do appear (DICTATOR, DONATE, HUNGER), but as items in a word-memory test.
3. **So Psych-101 can't teach sharing directly.** Whatever it does to the hunger sweep is transfer from "decide like a
   person in a bandit or a gamble". That was the point of the hold-out, but it also means the hold-out removes almost
   nothing about giving, because there is almost nothing there. This bears on the route decision; see Decisions.

## How a session becomes rows

A session is one person's whole experiment in English, each choice wrapped in `<<` `>>`. One row is one choice: the user
message is the instructions plus as much of the latest history as fits in 2,400 characters (older history is dropped
at a line break and marked `[...]`), with earlier choices left in as plain text; the assistant message is the choice.
Training uses mlx-vlm's `train_on_completions`, so the loss falls only on what the person chose. Two choices per
session, picked by a hash of (experiment, participant). The split is by person: a hash of (experiment, participant)
sends 5% of people to validation, so nobody is on both sides and a rerun writes the same bytes (tested).

The pilot sample is 40 rows per experiment (fewer where an experiment has fewer), chosen by hash and written round-robin
over the 72 kept experiments, so any prefix of the file is spread over all of them.

Measured with the tokenizer alone (no weights): pilot rows average 662 tokens (median 644, longest 1,189), 1.88 M tokens
in all 2,838. The whole `train.jsonl` is about 48 M tokens (from a fixed sample of 2,000 rows, mean 517.5). The raw
file is 3.26 characters per token, so about 257 M tokens, which agrees with the 253.6 M the paper reports.

## The pilot

**Not run yet.** Queued 2026-11-10 22:44:52 EST as `sens_lora_psych101_pilot`, priority 50, `--max-hours 1.5`,
`--gb 14`. What it will do when it gets the GPU (`train_pilot.py`, and its docstring):

- Base: `models/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct-4bit` (Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, 4-bit affine, group size 64, 5.76 GB).
- Adapter: LoRA rank 8, alpha 16 (scale 2), on `q_proj` and `v_proj` of the language model's 36 layers, no dropout.
  AdamW, learning rate 1e-4, batch 1, one pass, loss on the human's choice only (`train_on_completions`), seed 0.
- Data: the first rows of `lora/pilot/train.jsonl` (2,838 rows, 72 experiments, round-robin), Psych-101 revision
  `611565c`. Validation: the first 120 rows of `lora/pilot/validation.jsonl`, people the training never sees.
- **Sizing from a measured rate.** It trains 24 rows as a probe, reads tokens per second from the trainer's reports
  (dropping the first, which holds the warm-up), and takes
  `rows = tokens per second x 1,500 s / mean tokens per row`, capped by the 2,814 rows left and by 3,000. The sum it
  actually did is written to `run.json` as `training.sizing`. The rows average 662 tokens, so 2,838 rows is
  1.88 M tokens: the whole pilot sample fits 25 minutes at 1,250 tokens a second or better, and at half that it trains
  on about 1,400 rows. I have no measured rate yet because measuring one needs the GPU, which is the thing queued.
- **Fusing.** The sweep's backend runs full weights only, so the adapter has to be merged into the base. The job
  measures the validation loss four times: untrained, with the adapter, fused the stock way (rounded back to 4 bits),
  and from the fused folder after loading it again as an ordinary model. If the stock fuse keeps less than 90% of
  the adapter's gain, the 72 adapted layers are stored as 32-bit floats (their 4-bit base values plus the update) and
  the rest stay 4-bit.

Why the fuse gets that much care. On a layer-sized toy (random numbers, not the model: 1024 x 4096 weights, rank-8
update), measured tonight:

| Size of the update (RMS) | Error after fusing back to 4 bits, as a share of the update | A bfloat16 base fused in bfloat16 | float32 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00001 | 67.8 | 0.90 | 0 |
| 0.00005 | 13.6 | 0.54 | 0 |
| 0.0002 | 3.5 | 0.17 | 0 |
| 0.001 | 1.1 | 0.03 | 0 |

A share of 1 means the stored layer is as far from "base plus update" as if the update had never been added; above 1
means the fuse added noise bigger than the update. Two causes: one step between neighbouring 4-bit values (about 0.006
here) is far bigger than a small update, and MLX's 4-bit rounding isn't a fixed point (rounding already-4-bit weights
again moves them by 0.11 of a step on average). mlx-vlm's own `fuse(dequantize=True)` doesn't help: it adds the update
in the base's bfloat16, the third column. So `fuse_float` does the sum in float32. Both effects are pinned by
`FuseTest`, and the whole job (load, train, both fuses, write, reload, generate) runs on a tiny 4-bit Qwen-shaped model
through mlx-vlm's real loader in `PilotJobTest`. Whether the real pilot's update is small enough for this to matter is
exactly what the job's four losses will say; until it runs, that is a prediction from the toy.

## Decisions for Jayce

None of these is decided. For each: what is known, the options, and what each costs.

**1. What the licence means if Kokopelli is ever commercial.**
Known: the training file is Apache-2.0 on its card, which allows commercial use and puts conditions only on passing the
data on (keep the licence and notices, say what you changed). We don't pass it on. Not known: Psych-101 is a
compilation of 46 studies run by other labs, the repo carries no per-study terms and no LICENSE or NOTICE file, and I
didn't check each study's original release terms. The test set is a different licence (CC-BY-ND, gated, "do not
redistribute").
- (a) Take the card at its word. Normal for research; for a product it rests on the compilers having had the right to
  relicense everything.
- (b) Before weights trained on it ship in anything sold, check the original studies' terms, or ask the authors
  (contact on the card). Costs a few days once.
- (c) Use it for research only and train product weights on something else.

**2. Should the object-similarity experiment (`hebart2023things`) stay held out?**
It isn't about eating or sharing; it is "which of these three objects is the odd one out", and many objects are foods.
It is also a quarter of the dataset.
- (a) Keep it out (tonight's choice, because it can't be undone after training). The claim "the adapter never read
  about food" stays simple. Costs 24.4% of choices and 18.5% of sessions.
- (b) Put it back. The hold-out then costs 0.3% of choices. The adapter will have read "egg" thousands of times, in a
  task with no decision about food in it.
- (c) Drop only the triplets that name a food. Keeps most of it, needs a food-word list someone has to stand behind.
For the pilot the choice is nearly free either way (40 rows of 2,838).

**3. Is Psych-101 the right human data for this question?**
The plan's reason for this lane was "train on how people actually behave" about hunger and sharing. The file has no
experiment where anyone gives anything to anyone, and none about hunger. So it can only change the egg scene by
transfer from gambles, bandits and memory tasks. Also: the 4-bit base already comes out flat in the hunger sweep's
cue arm (`out/sensation/hunger_egg_base4bit/report.md`: +0 points [-7, +7] from full to starving), which is what
people do. There may be nothing here for human data to fix.
- (a) Keep Psych-101 as a general "decides like a person" prior and read the sweeps as transfer. That is what the
  pilot measures.
- (b) Add data that does contain giving under need (the studies behind `anchors.yaml` are the obvious start). Then
  the line between transfer and memory has to be redrawn by hand: train on some giving, hold out the hunger
  conditions.
- (c) Drop this route for the hunger question and keep it for the risk instruments the benchmarks area already has,
  where Psych-101 is rich (about half of its choices, 5.75 M of 10.68 M by my count of the study list, are gambles and bandits).

**4. How big a real run would be.**
Sizes measured tonight with the tokenizer (no weights): the file is about 257 M tokens (3.26 characters a token; the
paper says 253.6 M). After the hold-out, 48,874 sessions, 8,035,968 choices and about 209 M tokens remain. **The
training rate on this Mac is not measured yet** (the pilot hasn't had its turn), so the hours below are per 1,000
tokens a second; scale them by the pilot's `training.tokens_per_second` when it exists.

| Run | Tokens | Hours per 1,000 tokens/s | Choices learned from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tonight's pilot sample, once | 1.88 M | 0.5 | 2,838 (0.04%) |
| The prepared `train.jsonl`, once (2 choices a session) | about 48 M | 13 | 92,743 (1.2%) |
| Every kept choice as its own row | about 4,200 M | 1,170 (seven weeks) | 8.04 M |
| Every kept choice, read the way Centaur does: each session once, loss on every choice in it | about 209 M | 58 | 8.04 M |

The third row is why one-choice-per-row can't be the real format: each row re-reads its whole context to learn one
choice. The fourth needs one new piece, a dataset class that marks every choice span in a window of a session (the
trainer's loss already honours a `completion_mask` if the row brings one), and windows of a few thousand tokens,
because whole sessions run to 30,000 tokens and won't fit in memory here.
- (a) A night or two on the Mac with the prepared file as it is: cheap, but 1% of the choices.
- (b) Build the every-choice format, run it on the Mac over several nights (resumable).
- (c) The same on PACE. Not set up: `kokopelli/ops/cloud/pace.json` doesn't exist.
For scale, Centaur itself was a 70 B model trained for about five days on one large GPU (from memory of the paper).

**5. Fusing an adapter into 4-bit weights loses it. How should trained weights reach the sweeps?**
The benchmarks backend can only run a folder of full weights, so every trained arm has to be fused. On the toy in
"The pilot" above, fusing back to 4 bits destroys a small update and fusing in bfloat16 loses half of one at RMS
0.00005. The real pilot's four validation losses will say whether its update is in that range; they don't exist yet.
- (a) What this lane does: store the adapted layers as 32-bit floats, leave the rest 4-bit. Exact, costs about 2.6 GB
  of disk per arm, and the model then mixes precisions (slower by an amount not yet measured).
- (b) Teach the backend to apply an adapter without fusing. Nothing is rounded and nothing extra is stored. It is the
  benchmarks area's file (`backends/llm_model_backend.py` refuses adapters because `llm_model.ask` can't take one).
- (c) Train on the full-precision base instead. 16 GB in memory while training, and `homeostatic.export.fuse_into`
  then fuses in bfloat16, which on the toy still loses a good part of a small update. **That may already affect the
  homeostatic area's own exports**: its fuse test uses a float32 toy model, where nothing is lost. Not measured on
  its real weights; worth one validation pass before and after its next export.

**6. Guardrails on a trained arm.**
The benchmarks runner won't run IFEval or MMLU on any trained arm until `PREREG.md` is locked and the arm is in
`contrasts.yaml`; its own handoff recommends keeping that door closed. And `lm-eval` isn't installed.
- (a) Leave it closed: pilots get no guardrails. The only signal that the adapter broke something is the sweep's own
  invalid-reply count against the base's.
- (b) Open a labelled diagnostic mode in the benchmarks area (its decision 9). Lets training runs see guardrail
  numbers early; the cost it names is that looking at benchmark scores during training wears down "we don't optimize
  for any benchmark".
- (c) Lock the pre-registration with a `psych101` arm in it. That makes this a registered experiment, which is more
  than a pilot deserves.
Either (b) or (c) also needs a venv with `lm-eval[api,ifeval]` and `EVALS_LM_EVAL` pointing at it.

**7. Shared files.** `pyproject.toml` and `tools/fable/test_all.sh` each gained one path so `make test-all` runs this
lane's tests. Revert at merge time if you'd rather the folder were listed differently.
